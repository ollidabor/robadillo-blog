I'm a software engineer, ten-plus years in. My favourite kind of work is taking
a system that everyone is slightly afraid of and making it boring — scalable,
well-contracted, and easy for the next person to touch.

## What I've been doing

Most recently I was at **Etsy**, shipping APIs and push-notification
infrastructure for a marketplace with around 90 million buyers. Before that I
ran the e-commerce surface at **Kavak** — Car Purchase through Checkout —
across five-plus countries, growing from IC to leading a team of six.

I'm comfortable owning something end to end: the architecture, the messy
migration nobody wants, and the on-call pager that comes with it.

## A few numbers I'm proud of

- A BFF that unlocked a **$6M–$16M** annualised GMS initiative at Etsy
- **2M+** daily views across the Kavak e-commerce surface, five-plus countries
- An FCM → Braze migration with end-to-end experimentation, for ~90M buyers
- Engineers I mentored into senior roles, on teams of four to six

## How I like to work

<Callout>
Contracts first, then code. Most of the pain I've seen in distributed systems
wasn't in the algorithms — it was two teams with different ideas about what a
field meant.
</Callout>

I'd rather delete a service than add one. I write the migration plan before the
migration. And I think the best compliment a system can get is that nobody
talks about it.
